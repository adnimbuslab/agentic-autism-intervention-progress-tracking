# Gold Annotation Codebook

Decision rules for the source-level gold standard annotated in
`tests/eval/extraction/results/annotation_sheets/`. `VALUE_REFERENCE.csv` lists
*what the values are*; this file settles *where the boundaries fall* on the
three distinctions that produced the bulk of inter-annotator disagreement in
the first round.

The governing principle is unchanged from `INSTRUCTIONS.txt`: record only what
the transcript supports, and prefer `none` to a guess. These rules narrow what
"supports" means for three fields; they do not license inference beyond the
segment.

## 1. `modality` — gestural is communicative, not physical

`modality` records **how the child communicated**, not how the child moved.

`gestural` covers acts directed at a partner that carry a message:

    pointing, showing, giving, reaching to request, head nod/shake,
    waving, pushing away as refusal, leading an adult's hand

A physical action on an object -- picking up, opening, closing, moving,
stacking -- is **not** gestural. That action is the `behavior`; its `domain` is
`play`, `motor` or `adaptive`. An action-only segment with no communicative
signal takes `modality = none`.

The test is whether the act had an addressee. In CHAT, the evidence is the
`%gpx` gesture tier, a `%spa` code marking a communicative act, or `%act`
wording that names a partner ("shows mother", "points to the box"). An `%act`
line describing plain object manipulation is not gestural.

`multimodal` requires two channels *both carrying the message* -- speech plus a
communicative gesture. Speech accompanied by unrelated object handling is
`verbal`.

**`unknown` vs `none`.** `unknown` = the child communicated, but the transcript
does not show through which channel. `none` = there was no communicative act.

## 2. `outcome` — accuracy needs an identifiable target

`correct`, `incorrect`, `approximation` and `partial` apply only where the
transcript establishes a target the response can be judged against:

  - a question with a determinate answer
  - an instruction or demand
  - an imitation model
  - a request whose fulfilment is observably granted or refused

`no_response` applies where a target existed and the child did not respond.

Ordinary conversation with no accuracy criterion -- spontaneous comment, play
narration, social chat -- has no target: `outcome = none`.

Adult acknowledgement ("that's right", repeating the child's word back) is
positive evidence of both a target and of correctness. Absence of
acknowledgement is **not** evidence of incorrectness. Where a target existed
but its resolution is not visible in the segment, the value is `unknown`, never
`incorrect`.

This rule protects more than the gold set. `analytics_engine` consumes
`outcome` for goal-attainment and accuracy KPIs; scoring free conversation as
`correct` would inflate attainment against a criterion that was never set.

## 3. `prompt_level` — an adult turn is not automatically a prompt

`prompt_level` records **the support the child needed for the target
behavior**, not whether an adult spoke first. Conversation is dialogic. If
every preceding adult turn counted as a verbal prompt, `independent` would be
practically unreachable in these corpora, and `_independence_rate` would be
driven toward zero by the annotation protocol rather than by the children.

**Antecedent (not a prompt).** The turn that creates the occasion: a question
posed once, an instruction given once, a natural conversational turn. Target
behavior on that first presentation is `independent`.

**`gestural`** -- the adult points, motions or otherwise gestures toward the
target without supplying it verbally.

**`verbal`** -- verbal support added *after* the opportunity: the question
repeated or rephrased to narrow it, a hint ("it starts with h..."), "what do
you say?", a direct instruction to respond.

**`model`** -- the adult supplies the target response itself, whole or partial,
for the child to imitate. Model outranks verbal when the actual words are
given.

**`partial_physical` / `full_physical`** -- hand-over-hand or guided movement.

**`independent`** -- also whenever the child *initiates* the relevant behavior.
A preceding adult turn is context, not a prompt.

Where several prompts occur, record the most supportive level the child
actually needed. Hierarchy, least to most supportive:

    independent < gestural < verbal < model < partial_physical < full_physical

### Worked examples from the batch

`mark_flusberg_mark_070700.cha_seg_074` -- mother asks what the horse is
wearing, child says "mhm", mother re-asks, child answers "hat". The re-ask is
support added after the opportunity: `prompt_level = verbal`.

`rick_flusberg_rick_070013.cha_seg_007` -- the child spontaneously asks "play
this?". Child-initiated, so `prompt_level = independent` regardless of the
mother's preceding turn. Nothing is being tested, so `outcome = none`.

`roger_rollins_rollins_roger_rollins_030300.cha_seg_002` -- the child takes out
play-doh and tries to open the container. Object manipulation, not
communication: `modality = none`. The play is self-initiated:
`prompt_level = independent`. The closing instruction ("take the top off") is
not resolved within the segment: `outcome = none`.
